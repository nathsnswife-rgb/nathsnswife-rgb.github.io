ABOUT ROADSIDE STRANGE

Small team.
Big map. Very weird interests.

Roadside Strange is an independent project from DivineApps, a small husband-and-wife operation based in Arizona. It grew from years of genuine interest in paranormal investigation, local legends and the strange stories attached to real places.

LONG BEFORE THE APP

The paranormal was already part of our lives.

We lead our own paranormal investigation team, have investigated allegedly haunted locations, researched local legends and unexplained cases, and produced our own paranormal podcast.

That experience is a big part of why Roadside Strange exists. We already knew how scattered this information could be, and how much digging it sometimes took just to answer one deceptively simple question:

“What’s haunted near me?”

WHERE THE APP CAME FROM

Put the stories on a map.

Finding one strange location could mean bouncing between old websites, local-history pages, newspaper archives, folklore, maps and eyewitness accounts, then trying to work out where something actually happened and whether anyone could responsibly visit the area.

The first idea was simply a map of hauntings. Then it became obvious that the strange world does not respect neat categories. Cryptid reports belonged there. UAP cases belonged there. Weird historical events and local legends belonged there too.

Road trips followed naturally. Then saved case files, Found check-ins, achievements, comments and reviewed community submissions. Each feature grew from the same goal: make exploring strange stories feel like exploring, not homework.

Today, Roadside Strange brings those stories together in one place so a normal drive can turn into something a little less normal.

HOW ROADSIDE STRANGE WORKS

Where do the cases come from?

Research can include public records, historical material, newspapers, official sources, local-history collections, folklore, location-specific reporting and eyewitness accounts. Different kinds of evidence are treated as different kinds of evidence.

Are cases automatically scraped?

No. Cases are selected, reviewed, located and written for Roadside Strange rather than being automatically dumped onto the map from another database or feed.

How is folklore handled?

A legend can be worth preserving without pretending it is established fact. Folklore, reported encounters and documented history are not presented as though they all carry the same level of evidence.

What about private property?

Public access matters. Where an exact location is closed, unsafe or private, Roadside Strange uses a nearby public reference point, road, town or regional case instead of encouraging trespass. Current signs, closures and local rules always win.

Can users submit cases?

Eligible Premium users can submit cases, but submissions do not instantly become public. They go through review first so a new report is not automatically presented as verified.

Why DivineApps?

DivineApps is the small developer and business identity behind Roadside Strange. There is no giant studio behind the curtain, just a very small team and a very large list of strange places still to add.

THE RULE WE CARE ABOUT MOST

Curiosity without bullshit.

Roadside Strange does not need every story to be proven supernatural to be worth preserving. Folklore is part of a place. Eyewitness reports are part of a place. So are documented history, mystery, rumor and stories people have passed around for decades.

The job is not to pretend they are all the same thing. The job is to give each story useful context, keep the map responsible and let people decide what they make of it.

WHY KEEP DOING IT?

Because there is always another story.

Another haunted building. Another roadside legend. Another strange light in the sky. Another creature somebody swears they saw twenty years ago. Somewhere, somebody is still asking what is strange near them.

That is what Roadside Strange is for.
